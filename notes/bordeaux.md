# Bordeaux

## 1. The 1855 Classification: classified growths and their communes

> KEEP IN MIND: this is the first block of the Advanced syllabus point "All classified growths and their communes from each AC". Here we cover the 1855 classification (Médoc, Graves and Sauternes/Barsac). Saint-Émilion has its own, different classification and comes later.

### Words you need first

Before the lists, four words that the whole topic depends on. If these are clear, everything else is easier.

- **Château:** the wine estate (the producer). In Bordeaux a classification ranks *châteaux*, not vineyards or villages.
- **Cru / growth:** a ranked estate. "First growth" is the English for *premier cru*.
- **Commune:** the village or administrative area where the château is.
- **Appellation (AOC, also called AOP):** the legally defined wine area on the label. One appellation can cover several communes.

> EXAM TRAP: the syllabus asks for **commune**, not only appellation. Margaux is both an appellation and a commune, but Château Palmer is in the *Margaux appellation* and in the commune of *Cantenac*. Always learn both.

### What it is and where it comes from

- It was requested by Emperor Napoleon III for the Paris Universal Exhibition of 1855. The Bordeaux Chamber of Commerce gave the job to the syndicate of wine brokers, and the list was published on **18 April 1855**. ([CIVB](https://www.bordeaux.com/en/classifications/classifications-1855/), [Conseil des Grands Crus Classés en 1855](https://gcc-1855.fr/en/))
- The brokers ranked by two criteria: the **reputation** of each estate and the **market price** its recent vintages fetched. It was a ranking of what the market already believed, not a tasting. ([CIVB](https://www.bordeaux.com/en/classifications/classifications-1855/))
- It covers only **red wines of the Médoc** (plus one red from Graves, Haut-Brion) and the **sweet white wines of Sauternes and Barsac**. Other Bordeaux areas have their own classifications. ([CIVB](https://www.bordeaux.com/en/classifications/classifications-1855/))
- Reds have **five levels** (first to fifth growth). Sweet whites have **three** (Premier Cru Supérieur, Premier Cru, Second Cru). ([CIVB](https://www.bordeaux.com/en/classifications/classifications-1855/))
- Total: **61 red estates and 27 sweet white estates, 88 in all**. ([CIVB](https://www.bordeaux.com/en/classifications/classifications-1855/), [Conseil des Grands Crus Classés en 1855](https://gcc-1855.fr/en/))

> KEEP IN MIND: the classification is basically frozen. It has changed only once in a meaningful way: in **1973 Château Mouton Rothschild was promoted from second to first growth**. ([Decanter](https://www.decanter.com/learn/the-official-bordeaux-wine-classification-of-1855-41072/)) The CIVB says it "never changes", so the 1973 promotion is the exception you can mention in an exam answer.

> BY THE WAY: Wikipedia also records two minor adjustments: Cantemerle was added as a fifth growth in 1856, and Château Dubignon (a third growth) was absorbed into Malescot St-Exupéry. I could not find these on the official sites, so treat them as secondary-source detail. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordeaux_Wine_Official_Classification_of_1855))

### The numbers at a glance

| Level | Red (Médoc + Haut-Brion) | Sweet white (Sauternes + Barsac) |

### Sweet white wines: Sauternes and Barsac

Two appellations, five communes. **Sauternes** covers the communes of Sauternes, Bommes, Fargues and Preignac. **Barsac** is the fifth commune and has its own appellation. A Barsac estate may label its wine as Barsac or as Sauternes. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordeaux_Wine_Official_Classification_of_1855))

### In short (my summary for you)

> KEEP IN MIND: 1855 = **61 reds + 27 sweet whites = 88 estates**. Reds are ranked in five levels (5, 14, 14, 10, 18). Sweet whites in three (1, 11, 15). Margaux has the most (21), then Pauillac (18), Saint-Julien (11), then five each for Saint-Estèphe and Haut-Médoc, plus Haut-Brion. Only change: Mouton to first growth in 1973.
